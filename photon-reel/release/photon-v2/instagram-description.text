NO.002 — THE PULSAR

A lighthouse running at about 11 turns a second

Particles moving along curved trajectories shed gamma rays. Here, photons leave their calculated emission sites and travel straight while the source keeps rotating. Radiation emitted at different times builds a sweeping, curved pattern in space

The crosshair marks a fixed distant viewing direction; its position is schematic. Its flash and moving pulse trace use the original calculated photon arrival times, including light-travel delays

Invisible gamma rays, explicit false colors: blue 0.1–0.3 GeV, cyan 0.3–1 GeV, cream 1–3 GeV, orange 3–100 GeV. Colors blend according to calculated photon numbers. Small arrows show calculated photon directions, not magnetic-field lines

One 89.371 ms rotation takes 8 seconds here, repeated four times. The tiny central dot marks the neutron star

This Vela-informed calculation reuses a numerical force-free magnetosphere, particle trajectories, prescribed acceleration and curvature radiation. It is not a telescope image or a validated Vela reconstruction: the modeled spectrum and pulses still differ from measurements. Photon sampling, glyph size, exposure and the finite display window are visualization approximations
