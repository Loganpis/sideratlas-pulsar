NO.002 — THE PULSAR
@sideratlas
P = 89.371 ms; α = 75°; ζ = 65°

Rapid rotation sweeps gamma rays through space
Particles shed energy through curvature radiation
Photon directions and travel delays shape the received pulses
Blue waves and white markers show the same photon population

CALCULATED
Numerical force-free magnetosphere and particle trajectories
Laboratory curvature, acceleration–loss integration and 0.1–100 GeV photon yields
Emission directions, straight photon propagation and the original delayed observer pulse
r(t) = r_em + c(t − t_em)n
t_arr − D/c = t_em − n·r_em/c
R_LC = cP/(2π) ≈ 4,264 km

ASSUMPTIONS
One field snapshot rotates rigidly
Injection and acceleration are prescribed; no self-consistent pair plasma
Radiation follows particle velocity; no resolved finite beaming cone
Emission calculated over 0.7–2 R_LC; flat-space photon propagation
Enlarged numerical star; the tiny central dot is a position marker
Finite photon sampling, fixed exposure and a schematic distant observer
Blue → cyan → cream → orange codes increasing gamma-ray energy, not visible color
White markers highlight sampled photon directions, not extra emission
Pulse shape and spectrum differ from Vela; no validated reconstruction
32 seconds = four 8-second loops; one physical rotation per cycle
