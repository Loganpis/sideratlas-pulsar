NO.002 — THE PULSAR

Vela has a serious sense of rhythm: one turn every 89.371 milliseconds, about 11 times a second

Around this spinning stellar remnant, electric and magnetic fields guide particles moving close to light speed. Curved trajectories let them shed energy as gamma rays. Rotation, beaming and light-travel delays shape the pulses measured at Earth

This original, Vela-informed visualization maps the model's outer emission region in 0.1–100 GeV gamma rays. These invisible photons are translated into illustrative colors. One rotation takes 8 seconds here, repeated four times

The calculation combines a numerical force-free magnetosphere, particle trajectories, prescribed acceleration and curvature-radiation losses. It remains an approximation: the computed pulse shape and spectrum differ from Vela's measurements. The numerical star is enlarged, particles are injected at a finite radius with assumed loading, and one field pattern is rotated rigidly. The scene is an emission map, not a photograph or a validated reconstruction
