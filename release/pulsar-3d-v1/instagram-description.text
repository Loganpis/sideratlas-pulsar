NO.002 — THE PULSAR
@sideratlas
Original 3D model — a rotation-powered pulsar
P = 89.37 ms · M = 1.4 M☉ · R = 13 km
B_p = 3×10¹² G · T∞ = 0.70 MK
Magnetic axis tilted 75° from the spin axis
A compact star powers a rotating magnetosphere
Magnetic fields guide charged particles
Fast particles radiate in preferred directions
Rotation sweeps radiation like a lighthouse
r = L sin²θ · R_LC = cP/(2π) ≈ 4264 km
CALCULATED
Near-zone dipole geometry and its rotation
Stellar light from the existing approximate hydrogen atmosphere
Schwarzschild surface light bending and redshift, local Doppler shifts
ILLUSTRATED
Blue-white field curves reveal invisible magnetic geometry
Paired polar envelopes explain the lighthouse effect
Beam spread uses dipole tangents at an assumed radius of 6R
These are direction guides, not photon paths or optical radiance
Guide brightness, color, width profile and length are display choices
ASSUMED
Prescribed dipole; selected loops extend to 5.6R
True star-to-field scale in a close view, not the entire magnetosphere
Euclidean guide projection; no GR field distortion or global sweepback
Uniform fully ionized H atmosphere with simplified magnetic opacity
No new PIC, pair cascade, exact Vela fit or naked-eye prediction
Existing particle cooling and optical-emission calculations remain separate
One rotation slowed to 8 seconds, repeated four times
